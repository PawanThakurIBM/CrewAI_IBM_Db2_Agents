# Weather Operations Standard Operating Procedure

**Document ID:** SOP-OPS-004  
**Version:** 4.0  
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-15  
**Owner:** Chief Pilot / VP Flight Operations  

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines operating limits, decision processes, and coordination requirements when adverse weather conditions affect AirConnect flight operations. It applies to all phases of flight including ground operations, departure, en-route, and approach.

---

## 2. Weather Severity Classification

| Level | Description | Operational Impact |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| Low | Normal conditions, no restriction | Operations normal |
| Medium | Marginal conditions, enhanced monitoring | Increased fuel, alternate required |
| High | Near limits, special procedures | Possible delays, diversions likely |
| Severe | At or beyond operating limits | Operations suspended |

| Operation | Minimum |

| Landing visibility (CAT I) | 550 m RVR |
| Landing visibility (CAT II) | 300 m RVR |
| Landing visibility (CAT III A) | 175 m RVR |
| Maximum crosswind component | 29 knots (dry), 20 knots (wet), 15 knots (contaminated) |

| Maximum crosswind | 33 knots (dry), 25 knots (wet) |

## 4. De-Icing Procedures and Holdover Times

### 4.1 De-Icing Decision
De-icing is mandatory when:
- Frost, ice, or snow observed on critical surfaces
- Temperature ≤ +3°C with visible moisture (rain, drizzle, fog)
- Ground icing condition declared by airport authority

### 4.2 Anti-Icing Fluid Types and Holdover Times

| Fluid Type | Temperature Range | Holdover (Freezing Rain) | Holdover (Snow) |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| Type I (orange) | 0°C to -10°C | 4–8 minutes | 5–12 minutes |
| Type II (yellow) | -3°C to -14°C | 12–25 minutes | 18–35 minutes |
| Type IV (green) | -3°C to -14°C | 14–30 minutes | 22–45 minutes |

**Critical:** Holdover times are estimates. Crews must complete takeoff within the holdover time or request re-treatment. A pre-takeoff contamination check is mandatory.

## 5. Thunderstorm Avoidance Rules

- No aircraft may penetrate a cumulonimbus (CB) cloud
- Lateral separation from CBs: minimum **20 nautical miles** (10 nm at night)
- If CB is on departure or arrival path and cannot be avoided within fuel endurance, delay or diversion is mandatory
- Wind shear alerts from ATIS or SIGMET must trigger crew briefing before departure
- Dispatch must add 15% fuel buffer when convective SIGMET is active on route

---

## 6. Contaminated Runway Operations

### 6.1 Runway Condition Reporting
Runways are classified using RCAM (Runway Condition Assessment Matrix):

| RWYCC | Condition | Braking Action |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| 6 | Dry | N/A |
| 5 | Wet | Good |
| 4 | Wet / Slush | Good to Medium |
| 3 | Dry Snow | Medium |
| 2 | Wet Snow / Ice | Medium to Poor |
| 1 | Ice | Poor |
| 0 | Wet Ice | Nil |

Operations are PROHIBITED on RWYCC 0 and require Captain approval with performance calculation on RWYCC 1.

### 6.2 Performance Adjustments
For contaminated runways, dispatch must recalculate:
- Landing distance (apply 15% increase for RWYCC 2)
- Takeoff distance (apply 10–20% increase depending on condition)
- V-speeds adjusted in performance tool

---

## 7. Low Visibility Approach Procedures

- CAT II and CAT III approaches require both crew and aircraft certification
- CAT III approaches require airport Low Visibility Procedures (LVP) to be active
- During LVP, runway and taxiway holding short lines are mandatory — no vehicle crossings
- Additional crew rest required before CAT III duty (12-hour minimum rest)
- Auto-land is mandatory for CAT IIIA/B approaches

---

## 8. Operations Control Weather Monitoring

- Operations Controller checks METAR, TAF, and SIGMET every 30 minutes for active delays
- Forecast deterioration within 2 hours of ETD triggers automatic Duty Manager notification
- PIREPS (pilot reports) from preceding aircraft take precedence over automated forecasts for approach conditions
