# Passenger FAQ — Flight Disruptions

**Document ID:** FAQ-PAX-001  
**Version:** 2.2  
**Owner:** Customer Experience  

---

## Q1: What are my rights if my flight is delayed?

**A:** Your rights depend on the delay duration and route:

- **Under 2 hours:** You will be kept informed. No vouchers are required.
- **2–3 hours:** You are entitled to free meals/refreshments and access to communication (phone calls or Wi-Fi).
- **3–5 hours:** Enhanced meal vouchers and rebooking assistance if you have onward connections.
- **Over 5 hours:** You may choose a **full refund** instead of continuing your journey. If you continue and your arrival is delayed by 3+ hours, you may also be entitled to financial compensation (€250–€600 depending on route distance) if the delay was within the airline's control.

---

## Q2: Can I get a refund if I choose not to travel?

**A:** Yes. If your flight is delayed by 5 or more hours and you decide not to travel, you are entitled to a **full refund** of your unused ticket — including taxes and fees. This applies regardless of your fare type (even non-refundable tickets). Refunds are processed to your original payment method within 7 calendar days (EU) or as per DGCA rules for India travel.

If you are part-way through a journey (e.g., your connection is disrupted), you are entitled to a refund of the unused portion AND a return flight to your point of departure if continuing the journey has become pointless.

---

## Q3: What meals am I entitled to?

**A:** Meal entitlements are based on delay duration:

| Delay | What You Receive |
|-------|-----------------|
| 2–3 hours | Light refreshment or snack voucher |
| 3–5 hours | Full meal voucher (lunch or dinner) |
| 5+ hours | Full meal + subsequent meals for overnight delays |
| Overnight | Breakfast at hotel the following morning |

Vouchers are issued at the gate or service desk. If the airline fails to provide a voucher and you buy your own food, you can claim reasonable receipts (up to the voucher value) after travel.

---

## Q4: Will the airline pay for my hotel?

**A:** Yes, if your delay requires you to stay overnight at a city that is not your home. The airline must arrange hotel accommodation and transport to/from the hotel. You do not need to arrange it yourself — ask the airline's service desk.

If the airline fails to arrange a hotel, you may book your own and claim reimbursement for a **reasonable cost** (3-star equivalent; up to €100/night internationally). Keep your receipt.

---

## Q5: How do I claim financial compensation?

**A:** You can claim compensation:

1. **At the airport** — visit the airline's service desk before leaving.
2. **After travel** — submit a claim on the airline's website within the limitation period (2 years in India, 6 years in most EU countries).
3. **Via a claims management service** — they charge a commission, so direct submission is recommended.

To make a claim you will need:
- Your booking reference (PNR)
- Flight number and date
- Evidence of actual arrival time (boarding passes help)
- Your bank details for payment

AirConnect targets processing all claims within 14 days.

---

## Q6: What counts as extraordinary circumstances?

**A:** Extraordinary circumstances are events outside the airline's control that could not have been avoided even with all reasonable measures. Examples include:

**Qualifies as extraordinary:**
- Severe storms, hurricanes, heavy snow declared by meteorological authority
- Air Traffic Control (ATC) restrictions and airspace closures
- Airport security incidents ordered by government
- Political unrest or airspace closure due to conflict

**Does NOT qualify as extraordinary:**
- Technical problems that are part of normal aircraft maintenance
- Crew shortages from poor scheduling
- IT system failures
- Overbooking / commercial decisions

When extraordinary circumstances apply, meals and hotel accommodation are still owed — only financial compensation (€250/400/600) is waived.

---

## Q7: My connecting flight was missed because of the first flight. What happens?

**A:** If both flights are on the same booking (same PNR), the airline is responsible for getting you to your final destination. They must rebook you on the next available flight at no additional cost. If the wait causes an overnight stay, hotel and meals are provided.

If the flights are on separate bookings, the airline for the disrupted flight is only responsible for getting you to the connection city. Your second booking's airline has no obligation, and you may need to rebook at your own expense — this is why travel insurance is strongly recommended for self-connected itineraries.
