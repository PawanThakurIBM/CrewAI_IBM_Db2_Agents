# Crew FAQ — Flight Disruptions

**Document ID:** FAQ-CREW-001  
**Version:** 1.5  
**Owner:** Crew Operations / Chief Pilot Office  

---

## Q1: What happens to my duty time if the flight is delayed on the ground?

**A:** Your Flight Duty Period (FDP) clock **does not stop or pause** during a ground delay. It started at your sign-on time and continues running. If the cumulative FDP (sign-on to estimated block-in of the disrupted flight) is going to breach your maximum allowed FDP, you must notify the Captain and NCC as early as possible.

**NCC should be informed at T-3 hours before projected breach**, not when you're already at the limit. Early notification allows time for a crew swap to be arranged (typically 2–3 hour lead time).

---

## Q2: What if I feel fatigued during a delay? Can I call fatigue?

**A:** Yes. Crew fatigue reporting is **non-punitive** at AirConnect. If you feel your ability to safely perform your duties is impaired due to fatigue — whether from the delay, disrupted sleep, or any other reason — you must inform the Purser (cabin crew) or Captain (flight deck crew) immediately. Fatigue calls are protected under AirConnect's Just Culture policy and will not result in disciplinary action.

The Captain then reports the fatigue call to NCC and a crew swap is initiated. Never fly when fatigued.

---

## Q3: Who do I contact for a crew swap?

**A:** The crew swap process is:

1. Captain / Purser determines swap is required
2. Captain notifies NCC (Network Control Centre) via ACARS or phone
3. NCC crew scheduling desk arranges replacement crew
4. Station Manager at the airport handles logistics (transport, hotel if needed)

You do not contact crew scheduling directly — all swap requests go through the Captain to NCC.

---

## Q4: What do I tell passengers during a long delay?

**A:** Be honest, empathetic, and regular. Passengers need updates — silence increases anxiety.

Key guidelines:
- Make an announcement every 20–30 minutes (more frequently for delays > 3 hours)
- Use the approved scripts in SOP-OPS-001 as a base
- Never speculate on exact departure times unless confirmed by OCC
- Do not discuss internal operational issues (maintenance faults, crew issues) with passengers
- For compensation questions: direct passengers to the ground agent or service desk
- For medical questions: refer to on-board medical kit and, if needed, contact MedLink via radio

If a passenger is extremely distressed, involve the Purser and consider moving them to a quieter area of the aircraft.

---

## Q5: What are my responsibilities during a diversion?

**A:** During a diversion:

**Cabin crew:**
1. Follow Captain's PA instructions
2. Prepare cabin for landing at alternate airport (standard landing prep)
3. After landing: calm passenger concerns, provide regular updates via PA
4. Do not permit passengers to deplane until Captain announces clearance
5. Check on MEDA, UM, and WCHC passengers first
6. Maintain crew rest awareness — your FDP continues from original sign-on

**Flight crew:**
1. Notify ATC of diversion with reason, alternate airport, and souls on board
2. Contact OCC via ACARS
3. Declare emergency (MAYDAY/PAN-PAN) if safety is the cause
4. After landing: file diversion report and await NCC recovery plan
5. Do not re-depart without NCC and engineering clearance

---

## Q6: Are there limits on how many hours I can work in a week?

**A:** Yes. In addition to per-flight FDP limits, cumulative limits apply:

| Period | Maximum Flying Hours |
|--------|---------------------|
| Any 7 consecutive days | 60 hours |
| Any 28 consecutive days | 190 hours |
| Any calendar year | 1,000 hours (EASA) / 1,000 hours (DGCA) |

Crew scheduling monitors these automatically. If you believe you are approaching a limit, contact crew scheduling directly. It is your legal right to decline a duty that would breach cumulative limits.

---

## Q7: What if I'm stuck at an alternate airport overnight due to a diversion?

**A:** NCC arranges the following for diverted crew:

- Hotel accommodation (minimum 3-star, single room per crew member)
- Transport to hotel within 30 minutes of aircraft block-in where possible
- Per diem or meal allowance at the hotel
- Wake-up call and transport back to airport

Rest at hotel: minimum 8 hours from room key-in to NCC recall notification. Your revised sign-on (for the recovery flight) will be issued by crew scheduling.
