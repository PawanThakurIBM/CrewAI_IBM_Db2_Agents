## 1. Purpose

This manual section covers the maintenance-related decisions that Operations must make during flight disruptions. It includes MEL dispatch decisions, AOG procedures, ferry flights, and substitute aircraft acceptance.

---

## 2. Minimum Equipment List (MEL) Dispatch Decisions

### 2.1 What Is the MEL?
The MEL is a document that permits aircraft to be dispatched with specific equipment inoperative under defined conditions and for a limited time. It is derived from the Master MEL issued by the aircraft manufacturer and approved by the regulatory authority (DGCA/EASA).

### 2.2 MEL Dispatch — Decision Flow

When a maintenance fault is found before departure:

1. **Engineer identifies fault** → checks MEL for applicable item
2. **MEL Category:**
   - **Category A:** Repair within interval specified in MEL (days/flights)
   - **Category B:** Repair within 3 consecutive calendar days
   - **Category C:** Repair within 10 consecutive calendar days
   - **Category D:** Repair within 120 calendar days
3. If fault falls within MEL limits AND all required conditions/procedures are met → **aircraft may be dispatched**
4. If fault is NOT in MEL or exceeds interval → **Aircraft on Ground (AOG)**

**No Captain may be pressured to accept an aircraft against their airworthiness judgment.**

## 3. AOG (Aircraft on Ground) Procedures

When an aircraft is declared AOG:

### 3.1 Immediate Actions (T+0 to T+30 min)
1. Duty Engineer notifies Station Manager and NCC immediately
2. NCC activates aircraft substitution process
3. Engineering assesses repair duration:
   - < 2 hours: hold passengers at gate
   - 2–4 hours: activate short delay protocol
   - > 4 hours: activate substitute aircraft procedure

### 3.2 Substitute Aircraft Sourcing

NCC checks the following in order:
1. Same station — spare aircraft in the rotation schedule
2. Nearest hub — aircraft available for positioning
3. Wet lease — registered wet-lease operators on AirConnect's approved list
4. Fleet-sharing partner airline (with ACMI agreement)

**Target: substitute aircraft ready within 4 hours of AOG declaration.**

### 3.3 Aircraft Type Change Impact
If the substitute aircraft is a different type:
- Check seat capacity — may result in passenger offload if smaller aircraft
- Catering order must be revised to new aircraft type
- Crew type rating must be confirmed — crew may need to change
- Performance calculations re-run by dispatch for new aircraft

## 4. Ferry Flight Authorization

A ferry flight transports an unserviceable aircraft to a maintenance base without passengers (ferry with crew only).

### 4.1 Conditions for Ferry Flight Authorization
- Only authorised by Accountable Manager or Chief Engineer
- Special flight permit (SFP) from DGCA/EASA required if aircraft not fully airworthy
- MEL relief may be applied to allow ferry under specific conditions
- No passengers permitted on ferry flights

### 4.2 Ferry Flight Crew Briefing
- Captain briefed on specific limitations applicable to ferry
- NOTOC (Notification to Captain) completed with all deferred defects listed
- Fuel plan includes contingency for potential MEL-related performance degradation

---

## 5. Substitute Aircraft Acceptance Checklist

Before accepting a substitute aircraft for a disrupted flight:

- [ ] Aircraft type rating confirmed for assigned crew
- [ ] Valid Certificate of Airworthiness confirmed
- [ ] Maintenance release (Tech Log) checked — no open defects that affect despatch
- [ ] Fuel state checked — sufficient for flight + reserves + alternate
- [ ] Catering loaded for correct aircraft configuration
- [ ] Safety equipment (life vests, slides) serviceable and within inspection dates
- [ ] MEL check: no Category A items overdue
- [ ] Captain briefed and accepts aircraft

---

## 6. Fuel Contamination Procedures

If fuel contamination is suspected:
1. Fuel uplift halted immediately
2. Sample taken and dispatched to approved fuel testing laboratory
3. Aircraft fuelling suspended until test result received (typically 2–4 hours)
4. Fuel supplier notified and held accountable
5. If contamination confirmed: full defueling and refueling required — typically 4–6 hours

NCC notified immediately — delay or cancellation decision escalated.
